Coding Was Never My Goal

From an early age I had to deliver to be paid. I would meet the client in London and they would tell me what they wanted. I wrote it, delivered it and then sent the invoice. I loved those days but was too young and inexperienced to know their value. I feel very fortunate to have this mentality and not have to experience the loss of coding identity that many of my peers are experiencing now.

I don't ever remember having an ego or pride in my coding abilities. I had ego and confirmation bias about many things, IT and non-IT related, but not coding.

Creating something for the customer was always the satisfaction.

Software Developers Are Different

Over the last few years, I have had the real pleasure of connecting with a younger Software Developers. I came to realised how different they are in motivation and goals was and how many of them truly loved coding and building.

With that said, I have been very moved watching YouTube videos of Software Developes now in crisis who are grieving the loss of hand coding and creation. They are not alone and our profession is not alone in this brave new AI era. Identity crisis is very personal and those software developers are hurting. Many have been in the industry for years.

We Are Not Our Job

My message is we will survive, in IT or outside it - it is not the end. In the film Fight Club there is a famous scene, "You are not your job" - you can find it on YouTube.

Take time to grieve and realise that the five stages of denial, bargaining, anger, depression and acceptance are natural during this reset.

Share your experience with family, friends, your doctor, on YouTube, whatever. I guarantee someone will benefit from it.

If you prefer to go it alone then go it alone. There were times I could not face the pity on people's faces, or tell my closest that I had just failed another interview or was "lost" again at work. Do whatever it takes for you to reset. I am with you.

Surviving The Change

In my work journey I have survived five recessions, the networking era, the GUI and OO shifts, the internet bubble, the cloud era and the pandemic, and that is just work. In my personal journey I have survived homelessness, unemployment, addiction, marriage breakup, bankruptcy, the pandemic, children draining my savings and being stuck in the recent Dubai attacks. So am quite sure I will come through the AI era and so will you.

No More UK Coal Miners - Coping With Adversity

The UK coal mining industry was decimated in the 1980s by Thatcher's Britain. Whether it was union karma or destiny, whole towns and villages were put out of work. They fought hard and lost to keep their identity.

The coal miners' experience always gives me strength. I call on the film Fight Club and the scene in the film Champions where John Hurt visits the children's cancer ward, which proves a turning point. I look at the news reporting on TV and see people struggling, I notice homelessness around me, the worn-down faces of people in trams, people arguing and ranting. The list goes on but it helps me to re-balance.

This is my way and you will have yours, but my message is: whatever you do to cope with the change, you will survive.

Good Luck

The coder is dead 1980-2025, long live the Software Developer.
