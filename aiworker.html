Nothing new under the sun

We have been here before, a new technology requiring a new way of working with new tools.

Believe it or not, in the 1990s the use of Microsoft Office (Excel, Word etc) was seen as a premium productivity skill and would regularly appear as such in job adverts. As time went by these skills became mandatory and many recruitment companies would test job candidates before sending them to clients.

Finally, the skills became integrated into everyone's way of working and were just assumed.

A new way of working

With the introduction of Claude CLI and ChatGPT desktop we will see an increasing demand from employers for prospective candidates to use these specific tools to enhance productivity.

Many of you are already using them or something similar. This way of working will follow the same path as Microsoft Office that was mentioned earlier.

Interviews will start to test candidates on the use of these tools and live demonstrations may be required. Your future employer may test to see how efficient you are in your prompts and how much depth of knowledge you have with these tools. They may ask you about AI agents, context and cost management!

It will no longer be just about your domain knowledge but how you can leverage that knowledge with AI tools to maximise your productivity. Some call this prompt engineering but it is much more.

In time, the efficiency level between companies will normalise as all companies will demand AI enabled working. However, just now using these tools will be seen as a plus and knowing how to use them today may help you beat the competition.

What can you do

As always, the answer lies in targeted education.

Persuade your employer to get you a ChatGPT Pro or Claude Pro subscription and download the tools and start learning today from free blogs, YouTube courses etc.

Your energy should be spent on navigating the apps, enhancing prompt engineering skills and building AI agents, skills and context fit for your purpose.

If your employer is reluctant then you may need to make that investment in time and money yourself.

What about me?

Well, nearly everyone in the software development industry has had to adapt, including me!

Once upon a time, I was the quickest to adapt but as I have aged I choose carefully how I "burn" the time I have left. I am not "living and breathing" these tools but my skills are growing as I started early. Unfortunately so are the skills of my competition! It is a race and always has been!

I am not only using AI skills to assist me in software development but am now spending a lot of time developing technical AI solutions, so that in the future I can honestly label myself as an AI engineer. I am not there yet!

Don't Panic

The AI era has begun but don't panic!

You have time to adapt, especially if you are young in this brave new world.
