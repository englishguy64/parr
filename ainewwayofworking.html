The New Way of Working

The new way of working for Software Developers is to use Claude Code, Codex or another AI tool to generate code from English language specifications. These specifications should be written in such a way that they optimise token use and align with customer or stakeholder requirements.

After the code is generated the Software Developer or automated process will run a set of AI Agents (custom written or from templates) that can verify it for correctness. These Agents may verify Acceptance Criteria, Security, Compliance, Performance, Regulatory Requirements etc. When completed the next stage in SDLC (Software Development Life Cycle) can continue.

For those of you that have not worked with Waterfall (a specification design methodology) and don't understand why it was replaced by Agile, I invite you to do some research. Both Waterfall and Agile have their advantages and failings; I leave you to decide.

The current expectation is that this Way of Working will yield faster delivery at reduced costs, but it is not proven.

AI Agents Are Not Our Salvation

The standard software practice of "good enough", releasing software with reduced Features and Technical Debt to meet commercial pressures, is widespread. I have found it in nearly all places I have worked over my career. There are exclusions such as the safety industries, but recent disasters show they are not perfect.

The new practice of using non-deterministic AI Agents as verification for AI-generated code seems to be growing, with only basic human-in-the-loop checks owing to sheer code volume. A side effect is both soft and hard gated AI Agent failures will impose stricter psychological and technical expectations on the release decision. The end result of this being more iterations, which in turn will lead to extra token costs and later delivery.

Furthermore, the AI Agents themselves may be imperfect, just like test suites that happily test themselves without testing end user requirements.

For some years a new strange breed of developer has risen believing no testers or QAs were needed. These same developers will now be in charge of verification. Eventually, commercial pressure will yield to them skipping the AI code verification leading to greater than or the same Technical Debt as before.

Too flat to fail?

In the UK Football Premiership teams have large squads for player injuries because without a deep squad results will suffer. When my father started as a food delivery driver for a major UK supermarket he spent two years as a reserve driver, but was used nearly every day.

With flatter organisations comes single points of failure and lack of collective judgement. In my domain some of these points are the Hero Developers. These are people that cannot be sick, be fired or be allowed to leave as they have too much vested knowledge or skill. This particular example leads to inflationary salary pressure, delayed or late starting projects (when they are sick).

Everyone talks about "flattening" and "more for less". It is these mantras and actions that are executed without fully understanding the consequences. Overly flat organisations lead to sickness, project delays, customer mistrust and ultimately reduced profits. The very things flattening is meant to prevent!

Having said that, corporate bloat is also a problem with company drones attending meaningless meetings and undertaking "looking busy" activities.

Resource constraint is here to stay

The normalisation of AI-enabled working (all companies employ it) will lead to the return of unrealistic expectations and resource constraints. It may take time for companies to realise that the productivity ceiling is not there once they start experiencing the side effects of flatness.

We may see an increase in employment to rebalance this, the only issue being who will still have the skills and what will they cost?
