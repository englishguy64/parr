AI era is here

The AI era is here and there is a lot of chaos and innovation happening. There is great uncertainty and a lot of fear, not just in IT.

In recent years, software development had become tediously grinding, but I accept that may have something to do with my longevity, the journeyman projects and bloated project management environments I often found myself in. To my joy, a set of AI-assisted coding tools gave me a second honeymoon, suddenly I was more creative and productive but I learnt from my internet days "there is no such thing as a free lunch". Eventually escalating token costs and unrealised productivity gains jolted the budget holders out of their AI delusions.

The Delivery Reality

From my experience, employers or customers are only interested in delivery. They are not interested in AI, code, tools, charming manners or what the salesman promised. So any activity that does not contribute to delivery must be eliminated. As I started to tune my approach to this reality, which meant cutting out meaningless meetings, tasks and activities, my unpopularity and black-sheep identity began to rise. However, I have to acknowledge that for many of my ex-colleagues the meaningless was meaningful, so navigating them and championing my cause was mainly wasteful. There are grey areas, so don't take my preaching too seriously. For example, if you laugh together and that bonds the team then it contributes indirectly to delivery.

Industry Crisis

Today many traditional software developers and IT professionals are going through an identity crisis asking questions like "Who am I now?" and "Will I lose my job eventually?" Yes, you may lose your job, but so did software developers in pre-AI days and recessions. These anxieties are normal, but worriers will fear the worst and optimists will hope for the best. To the worriers I say carry on regardless as distraction reduces performance!

Is there life outside IT?

As well as spells of unemployment both forced and self-inflicted I had a six-year IT time-out. It may not be what I wanted or planned but I survived and so will you. After my time-out I returned to IT but it was not a 'thank god' moment but a choice, although I have spent many years telling people I had no choice! There are very few things in life we have no choice over and nearly all of them we will survive.

The trust issue

The missing piece of the AI end-to-end delivery puzzle is trust. We do not trust AI tools to deliver complex production-ready software without some human in the loop. Sure, we can vibe apps and proofs of concept, but that is not what I am talking about. If we think of embedded software in aeroplanes, lifts, medical equipment, etc., it may be a long time before these industries "trust", but others will get there first depending on the tolerance levels and risk profile of the players.

Don't Panic

The AI era has begun, but don't panic because there are a lot of people out there doing that for you. We have a long way to go before a clearer picture emerges. We have been here before with the internet, cloud and low-code tools eras.

My message is this. You can always start again. Imagine if you started playing the piano tomorrow and continued for five years regularly. Consistency is key; some call it hard work. For someone who naturally procrastinates outside of IT I can tell you even a little consistency provides fantastic results. It really is 'Just Do It' or 'Just Try It'.
