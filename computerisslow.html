It's running slow, today

We have all heard it or experienced it at some time or another. After the 100th time the level of frustration can only be beaten by call centre hold and final cut-off. The feeling of IT rage is real.

So why does this happen so often? Below I have listed some simple explanations

Computer, Mobile or Device

It is just out of date, has not been restarted for a long time or something is not connected or broken.

Remedy: Restart it, get it serviced, upgrade it, restart it.

Local or mobile network

Too many people in the company are using the local network. This is often true at peak times, even when internet capacity is plentiful. An analogy is the motorway: you cannot use its speed if you cannot get on it.

While IT centres can operate what is known as dynamic scaling, they will not scale to an unlimited level just to maintain quality of service. The reason is not technical but very often economic.

Remedy: Reboot your router, wait it out, note the times and dates and send them to your manager, upgrade your network.

Internet Service Provider (ISP)

If the application or data is somewhere on the internet your request will need to travel to get to it. Any point on the route there can be delays.

Remedy: Wait it out, restart the local router if you can, note the times of day and raise it with your manager, upgrade your ISP connection, change your ISP.

Cloud Provider (Azure, AWS, GCP)

If the application you are trying to access or its data is on a cloud computer (you may not know that), then it may be busy because it has been given limited resources.
Remedy: Report the times and dates to your manager, horizontally or vertically scale the cloud resources.

Application

Even when you know you have the correct infrastructure you can be defeated at the last hurdle by the application running on the application servers.

1. Hard licence constraints prevent you from accessing a free licence if available.
2. The software cannot scale even if the hardware can.
3. You are being rate limited or denied access in favour of higher-value customers. This is common practice in subscription businesses with multi-tier plans.
4. The application is legacy and is not able to handle growing workloads, in the worst case hitting timeout limits.
Remedy: Add more licences, patch the application software using modern architectural practices, upgrade your plan.
